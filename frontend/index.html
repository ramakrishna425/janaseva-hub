PLAY AND MOVE

Simple age-appropriate activities

  • Birth–2 years: Talk, sing, respond to sounds, show safe high-contrast pictures, supervised floor play and gentle movement. Follow the child's cues.
  • 2–3 years: Picture naming, stacking large blocks, sorting large safe objects, action songs, simple pretend play and short outdoor walks with an adult.
  • 3–6 years: Drawing, clay/playdough with supervision, storytelling, matching games, dancing, hopping, ball play and simple nature observations.
  • School age: Outdoor games, cycling with safety gear, stretching, age-appropriate sports, reading, art and short movement breaks during study.

Keep activities playful and inclusive. Adapt them to each child's abilities. Avoid small objects that can cause choking, sharp tools, forced exercise or unsupervised water play.

NOTICE AND SUPPORT

Why children may feel stressed

  • Exam pressure, too much homework or fear of making mistakes.
  • Bullying, friendship difficulties, loneliness or feeling left out.
  • Family conflict, high expectations or frequent comparison with others.
  • Not understanding lessons, learning difficulties or lack of sleep.
  • Too little play, too many demands or upsetting online experiences.

What adults can do

  • Listen calmly without scolding; ask what is worrying the child.
  • Keep regular sleep, meals, play and study-break routines.
  • Make expectations realistic and praise effort, not only marks.
  • Talk with the child's teacher or school counsellor when needed.
  • Seek qualified professional help if distress persists or affects daily life.
For parents and caregivers

For babies and young children, use adult-supervised, play-based interaction. Screen media should not replace sleep, movement, conversation or play. Never leave a child alone with online content. If a child has persistent distress or stress that affects daily life, seek support from a qualified professional or trusted school counsellor.
